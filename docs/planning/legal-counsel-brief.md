# Legal Counsel Brief — payment rail, wallet classification and tax

Everything below blocks the launch of in-platform payments. None of it can be resolved by
engineering; the code has been built so that either answer is implementable, but the answers
themselves must come from counsel.

**Counsel profile needed**: US money-transmission / fintech regulatory, with adult-industry
familiarity. State sales-tax on digital goods and international VAT can be a second engagement.

**Status**: not yet engaged.

## 1. Money transmission — the structural question

We have deliberately designed the fan wallet as closed-loop to stay outside money transmission.
We need that reading confirmed or corrected.

1. Does accepting USDC and crediting a **closed-loop, non-withdrawable, non-transferable** prepaid
   balance spendable only on our own content constitute money transmission under FinCEN's rules or
   under state MTL regimes? Does the "closed loop" / "prepaid access to the issuer's own goods and
   services" exclusion apply?
2. Does **paying creators** change the analysis? We receive money from a fan, keep 20%, and remit
   80% to a third party (the creator). That is the classic agent-of-the-payee question. Which
   states recognise a payment-processor or agent-of-payee exemption that would cover it, and which
   do not?
3. Does receiving **cryptocurrency** rather than fiat change (1) or (2) in any state? Several
   states now regulate virtual currency transmission separately.
4. Is **FinCEN MSB registration** required, and if so does that pull in an AML programme, a
   compliance officer, SAR/CTR obligations and independent testing?
5. If a state MTL is required somewhere, what is the cheapest compliant path — geo-restricting
   that state, restructuring the creator payout as something other than a remittance, or
   registering?
6. Are there **BSA/OFAC screening** obligations on the fan side, the creator side, or both, given
   we already block sanctioned jurisdictions by IP but do not screen names?

## 2. Tax point of a wallet top-up

This one has an engineering deadline attached: the data model reserves tax fields at **both** the
top-up and the consumption event, because whichever one is correct, the other cannot be
reconstructed after the fact. We need the answer before real money flows, not after.

1. Is a wallet top-up a **single-purpose voucher** (tax due at top-up) or a **multi-purpose
   voucher** (tax due at redemption)? Our balance can buy subscriptions, PPV unlocks and tips —
   are those the same tax treatment, and does the tip change the answer?
2. Under US state sales tax on digital goods, is the taxable event the top-up or the purchase?
   Does the gift-card / stored-value treatment apply?
3. Are **tips** taxable supplies at all, or gratuities outside the tax base? If a tip is a
   gratuity, does routing it through a prepaid balance change that?
4. When we eventually open EU payments under Non-Union OSS, does the single/multi-purpose voucher
   analysis flip?

## 3. State sales tax nexus

1. Which of the 26 states we will serve tax digital content sales, and at what economic-nexus
   thresholds?
2. Does the closed-loop wallet create a nexus event at top-up in any state even where the content
   sale would not?
3. Do marketplace-facilitator rules apply to us? We are arguably facilitating sales by creators,
   which in several states would make **us** liable to collect on the full amount rather than on
   our commission.
4. What is the minimum viable monitoring — we currently plan to record buyer state and amount from
   the first transaction and alert on threshold approach.

## 4. P2P off-ramp

1. What is our exposure converting USDC to fiat through **Bitpapa / Bybit P2P** counterparties
   rather than a licensed exchange? Specifically: tainted funds received from a counterparty,
   inability to evidence source of funds, and counterparty AML failure.
2. Is there a due-diligence standard we should apply to counterparties, and should we cap
   per-counterparty and per-day volume?
3. Does the P2P route jeopardise the **Paxum** relationship — will Paxum accept inbound fiat from
   P2P counterparties, or does it need to originate from a named exchange?
4. What documentation should we retain per conversion to survive an audit or a Paxum review?

## 5. No corporate bank account

This is the least explored area and possibly the most operationally dangerous.

1. Can a US LLC lawfully operate with **no bank account**, receiving all value in crypto and
   holding fiat only in Paxum?
2. How does the LLC **pay federal and state tax** with no bank account? Does Paxum support ACH out
   to the IRS/state, or is an EFTPS-linked bank account effectively mandatory?
3. How are ordinary operating expenses (VPS, Didit, contractors) paid, and what does that do to the
   corporate-veil analysis?
4. Does the absence of a bank account itself trigger scrutiny — for example when filing, or when
   Paxum re-reviews the account?
5. What is the realistic timeline and cost to get a bank or crypto-friendly banking partner that
   accepts an adult-industry LLC, and should that be started in parallel regardless?

## 6. Creator classification and reporting

1. Are creators independent contractors for whom we must issue **1099-NEC / 1099-K**, and at what
   thresholds? Does paying in fiat via Paxum change the form?
2. Do we need **W-9 / W-8BEN** collection before first payout, and is our Didit KYC record
   sufficient identity evidence to support it?
3. Non-US creators: withholding obligations, treaty considerations, and what we must collect.

## 7. Adult-industry specifics

1. **18 U.S.C. §2257** record-keeping: our obligations as a platform hosting user-generated adult
   content, and whether our current `/2257` page and creator KYC records satisfy them.
2. Do any of the 26 age-verification states impose obligations on the **payment** flow separately
   from the content flow?
3. Is our decision to exclude Tennessee (rather than comply with the 60-minute re-verification and
   Class C felony exposure) defensible, and does IP-level blocking suffice as a defence?

## What engineering has already done to keep both answers open

- Fan wallet is **closed-loop in the Terms and in the product** — there is no withdrawal or
  transfer path for fans, and no code that could create one.
- The ledger records **buyer country and region** on every consumption order, so a nexus analysis
  can be run retrospectively over real data.
- Tax fields exist on **both** `payment_orders` (top-up) and `consumption_orders` (spend), left
  null until counsel picks the taxable event.
- Creator earnings are held as **pending** and only released in explicit batches, so a payout
  freeze pending advice costs nothing.
- Payments are restricted to the US at the jurisdiction layer, so the EU VAT question does not
  become live until we choose to make it live.
